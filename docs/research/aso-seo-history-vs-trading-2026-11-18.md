# Free growth check: Duckumentary (history) vs Paper Duck (trading), App Store search + Google (Nov 18, 2026)

Question: which duck course app can grow **mainly for free** through App Store search (ASO) and Google (SEO), with money as the #1 goal?
Builds on `history-app-2026-11-18.md`, `trading-niche-2026-11-18.md` and `skill-pick-2026-11-18.md`. Nothing from those is repeated except where a number is needed.
**Budget:** AppKittie **80 credits** (log 1,663 → 1,743, cap 1,743): 8 new keywords. DataForSEO not used. Everything else was free (iTunes Search, Wikipedia pageviews, Google autocomplete, web search, Similarweb free pages).
**Read every revenue/download number as a model estimate.** AppKittie can be ~10× off for small apps; Similarweb/Semrush visits are modelled too.

## 1. Short answer
- **Pick Duckumentary.** Head to head: **ASO history 7 vs trading 5.5 · SEO history 7.5 vs trading 3 · combined (ASO 60%, SEO 40%) 7.2 vs 4.5.**
- **App Store:** history has bigger winnable keywords and the apps on top are weak and old. "history quiz" (81/27) is led by an app last updated **July 2022** with 2.9k ratings. Trading's big words ("trading", "crypto", "stock market") belong to Robinhood, Webull, TradingView and Coinbase (340k–4.9M ratings) and can't be won. Its winnable words are small (24–39 popularity).
- **The honest catch:** trading users pay more. The App Store money sitting under trading's small keywords (simulators, ~$94k/mo across 8 apps) is bigger than under history's quiz keywords (~$55k/mo across 6 apps). Paper Duck would need fewer users to earn the same.
- **Google:** history is one of the best free-traffic topics there is. 30 history Wikipedia articles drew **74.6M** views in a year, against **4.9M** for 23 trading articles: about **12× more per article**, and ~25× more if you leave out Bitcoin and crypto. Small sites (TheCollector, History Oasis, WorldAtlas, History Skills, Nibble's blog) rank on page 1. Trading results are wall-to-wall brokers and finance publishers, and Google treats money advice as "Your Money or Your Life" content that needs proven expertise.
- **The form that survives Google's AI answers is "play", not "read":** quizzes, a daily guess-the-year game, printable trivia question lists and interactive timelines. 76% of the 25 history people and events we tested have a full "X quiz" long tail in Google autocomplete, against 40% for trading concepts.

### 2.3 Can a newcomer reach the top 3 in 3–6 months?
- **History: yes, for 4 of the 5 best keywords** (history quiz, world history, history trivia, learn history #2). Proof: 2026 newcomers with **under 40 ratings** already sit at #1–3 for "world history" and "ancient history", and Timeline Quiz (33 ratings) is #2 for "learn history". You need relevance (the words in the title and subtitle) plus a steady trickle of ratings, about 200–500 at 4.7★+, which a TikTok audience can supply. "history" itself (Paladin, Khan) is a 12-month-plus goal.
- **Trading: yes for "paper trading", "stock simulator", "trading simulator" and "day trading simulator"; no for every big word.** Proof: Day Trading Simulator (May 2024, 1.3k ratings) is #1 for "day trading" and #2 for "trading simulator". Tradicted (2025, 147 ratings) is #4. But MarketSim (20.8k) and Forex Trading School (11.4k) set a higher ratings bar than anything in history quizzes.

### 3.4 Google's rules
- **"Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL):** Google's quality rater guidelines hold pages that could hurt someone's finances to the highest standard of experience, expertise and trust. Raters look for credentials. An anonymous duck teaching day trading starts with a handicap, and every page needs a "not financial advice / simulated money" frame. History is low-stakes: a wrong date costs nothing, so E-E-A-T is earned with sources on every page (our planned sources card).
- **AI Overviews (AIO):** they appeared on ~16–25% of US searches in 2025 (Semrush, Ahrefs), mostly informational ones (~98% of AIO keywords are informational, Ahrefs). Pew (Mar 2025 data): people click a result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary vs **15%** without, and only 1% click a link inside the summary. Investopedia's visits reportedly fell from ~40M to ~27M a month (Loopex estimate); Similarweb now shows ~10.7M.
  - **Eaten:** "what is short selling", "doji meaning", "rsi explained", "who was Diogenes", "why did Rome fall": one-answer definitions. That's most of trading's education searches and part of history's.
  - **Survive:** things you do, not read: quizzes, daily games, printable question lists for trivia night or class, interactive timelines and maps, and story pages with a video and a hook the summary can't give. Most of history's long tail can be served this way. Trading's can't, beyond a chart-guessing game.

### 3.5 Site plan for Duckumentary (duckumentary.com or a /history section on a Grasp Labs site)
| Page type | Template | Survives AIO? | Count at 6 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interactive quiz** | `/quiz/{topic}`: 10 questions, instant answers, score card, "get 3 a day in the app" | Yes | 40 (eras, empires, figures, wars) |
| **Daily game** | `/daily`: guess the year from 3 clues (Whendle/Yeardle style), shareable emoji grid | Yes | 1 page, new puzzle daily |
| **Trivia question lists** | `/trivia/{topic}-questions`: 50–150 Q&As, printable, by difficulty | Mostly | 15 |
| **Story page** | `/stories/{slug}`: the lesson's drawn cards, 60 s duck video, myth-check, 2+ sources, 3-question quiz | Partly (video + quiz carry it) | 60 (one per lesson) |
| **Figure card page** | `/cards/{figure}`: the collectible ("Julius Quacksar"), 10 checked facts, quiz link | Partly | 30 |
| **Timeline** | `/timeline/{topic}`: interactive (Roman emperors, WWII, Egypt dynasties) | Yes | 5 |
| **Themed "on this day"** | `/on-this-day/{month}-{day}`: **one** duck story per date + quiz, not a list | Weak (onthisday.com and Britannica own lists) | later |
- **Programmatic, but checked:** the quiz, trivia and card templates are filled from the same JSON the app ships (one fact base, one sources list). Never publish an unchecked page: thin AI pages at scale are what Google's spam rules target, and wrong facts are Paladin's #4 complaint.
- **Internal links:** every page ends in a "Play today's story in the app" card (App Store smart banner on iPhone); story → figure card → quiz → era hub → `/daily`. Each era hub links its matching custom product page URL so web visitors land on the right store page.
- **Second money line:** quiz and trivia sites earn from display ads (Sporcle, JetPunk). Journey by Mediavine now accepts sites from ~1,000 tier-1 sessions a month (lowered in Jan 2026; check before applying). Optional, and only on the web, never in the app.

## Caveats
- App Store search counts aren't public. Popularity scores are relative, and "demand we can capture" comes from the downloads of apps already ranking, which include their ads.
- iTunes Search API order is close to, but not identical to, the App Store app's results. Ranks move daily.
- The web search tool is not Google, and it can't show AI Overviews. AIO rates come from 2025 studies.
- Wikipedia views measure curiosity, not Google volume. Trading searchers may skip Wikipedia for broker pages, which flatters history somewhat. The ~12× gap is too big to explain away, though. One trading title (Paper trading) is a redirect and one (Stock market simulator) was rate-limited, so neither is counted.
- Similarweb's free page labels some visit figures "last 3 months". I've read them as the latest monthly figure where Semrush agrees (Sporcle). Treat all visit numbers as ±50%.
- AppKittie revenue misses web sales and can be ~10× off for small apps.
