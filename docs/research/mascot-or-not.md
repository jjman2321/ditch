# Do we need a mascot? (Ditch, Nov 9 2026)

The owner asked: "Do we even need a mascot, or does it not work for our niche?" This doc answers that from our buyer's point of view and our chances of making money.

## Summary (read this if nothing else)
1. **Who pays for a subscription tracker:** iPhone users roughly 23–40, a little more often women than men, mostly earning over $50k. Something annoyed them first: a charge they forgot, a free trial that rolled over, or a total that turned out much higher than they guessed (people guess $86/month, the real number is $219).
2. **How they feel:** a bit guilty, a bit annoyed, and they want control. They aren't looking for a pet. They want something that **reliably** stops them losing money.
3. **The top-earning money apps don't use mascots.** On Mobbin, Rocket Money, Copilot and Monarch onboarding have no character at all (checked screen by screen). The rest of the top-grossing US finance list (YNAB and others) also shows none as far as we know, but that part wasn't checked screen by screen, so re-check it before quoting it.
4. **The finance apps that do have a mascot keep it small:** Dave's bear peeks in on the splash screen and then mostly disappears. Cleo has no body, only a voice. Full-time characters show up in kids' money apps (Greenlight, Kit) and in game-like apps (Duolingo, Finch, BitePal).
5. **Research:** people judge companies mainly on whether they seem **competent**, and that's what decides whether they buy (Aaker 2010). Cute characters mostly make a brand feel **warm**. Playful cuteness has even been shown to make people *more* likely to treat themselves (Nenkov & Scott 2014), the opposite of what Ditch is for.
6. **Characters really do help memory in ads** (System1/IPA: about 22–23% more likely to produce very large profit or share gains). That needs years of consistent ads, though, and Ditch has no ad budget.
7. **AI-made look is a real risk:** 47% of people say their trust drops once they realise something was made by AI. 18–28 year olds punish brands for it most (59%). Our raccoon is a GPT-Image 3D render.
8. **Our users mostly meet us through notifications.** A reminder on the lock screen shows our icon and our words, never a mascot. The thing people see most is our *voice*.
9. **The raccoon isn't something only we could have:** BitePal already owns "raccoon in an app". A "ditch" stamp, our real-logo cards and the total reveal belong to us.
10. **Pick: D, a signature "Ditched" stamp/flick plus voice (score 8/10).** Take the raccoon out of onboarding, the paywall, the widget and the screenshots. It can come back later as a small, hand-finished drawing in empty states, if testing shows people miss it.

  - age 23–34+

- **People who don't link a bank** (our exact niche) tend to be privacy-minded iPhone users. Bobby is the favourite in Reddit threads because it needs "no bank linking, no email scanning, no sign-in" ([Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/subscription-tracker-apps-data-privacy), a competitor's blog, so biased). They are picky about design and allergic to anything that feels cheap.

### Their emotional state, in plain words
- **"I got caught."** A trial rolled over, or a gym charge they forgot about. They feel guilty, a little embarrassed, and annoyed at themselves and at the company.
- **"I want control back."** What they want most is reliability. Bobby's biggest complaint in reviews is reminders that don't fire ("notifications randomly stop working", [AppsHunter reviews](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1059152023)). They aren't asking for fun. They're asking us to **never miss a charge**.
- **Tired of being nickel-and-dimed.** They're sick of subscriptions, so they're suspicious of a subscription app that feels pushy or gimmicky.

**What "premium" means to this buyer** (Tim Gabe's question): **calm and sharp**, not cosy and cute. Think "a smart friend who's good with money and has your back", not "a pet you look after".

### The pattern

## 3. What the research says

### Warmth vs competence
- People judge companies on two things: **warmth** (are you on my side?) and **competence** (can you actually do it?). This comes from the "Stereotype Content Model".
- **Aaker, Vohs & Mogilner (2010)** found that for companies, **competence predicted willingness to buy better than warmth did**. Firms seen as warm-but-less-competent lost sales ([paper PDF](https://carlsonschool-d8.prd.umn.edu/sites/carlsonschool.umn.edu/files/2019-04/164337_0.pdf), [Stanford summary](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/news/research/aaker_warm.html)).
- **Aaker, Garbinsky & Vohs (2012)**: the best place is the "golden quadrant", high on both. You get there by adding warmth *on top of* clear competence ([PDF](https://carlsonschool-d8.prd.umn.edu/sites/carlsonschool.umn.edu/files/2019-04/aaker_garbinsky_vohs_2012_jcp.pdf)).
- **What this means for us:** our product promise is pure competence ("your reminders will fire"). Warmth should be a seasoning, not the main dish.

### Cuteness
- **Cute pushes "warm", cool pushes "competent"** (Baoku et al. 2021, a study of digital products, [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/APJML-06-2021-0413/full/html)).
- A 2026 paper says some finance and tech brands *do* pull off cuteness, but only the **quirky, funny** kind, not the baby-faced kind, and the results are "often mixed" and can backfire ([Frontiers in Psychology 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1730776/full)).
- **Nenkov & Scott (2014), "So Cute I Could Eat It Up":**
  - After seeing playfully cute things, people **treated themselves more** (they took more ice cream with a cute scoop).
  - The authors say this could spill over into "savings, debt repayment".
  - ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/05/140521133258.htm), [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-676581.html))
  - To be fair: it was tested on food and objects, not apps. It's a warning sign, not proof.

### Human-like helpers in banking
- Studies of banking chatbots find that a human-like **way of talking** raises trust, but only when it also comes across as competent and communicates well. Some studies also find it raises privacy worries ([EMAC 2025](https://proceedings.emac-online.org/pdfs/A2025-125836.pdf), [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/b/ris/abemcp/023219.html)).
- None of these studies separates how a character *looks* from how it *talks*.
- **What we take from it:** personality in the words (Cleo-style) is the safer form of "human" in money apps.

### Characters and memorability
- **The good news for mascots:**
  - System1, with Peter Field, studied ~300–490 long-running ad campaigns since 1992. Campaigns with a recurring character ("fluent device") were **23% more likely to get very large market-share gains and 22% more likely to get very large profit gains** ([System1](https://system1group.com/blog/creating-memorable-characters-in-advertising), [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/research-by-system1-shows-memorable-characters-in-ads-boost-chances-of-profit-by-30)).
  - In insurance, the GEICO gecko has 94% recognition ([Carrier Management](https://carriermanagement.com/news/2015/03/19/137011.htm)).
- **The catch:**
  - These are **TV and ad campaigns run for years** with big budgets.
  - System1 itself says characters work through "consistency of tone" over a decade ([System1](https://system1group.com/blog/the-seven-habits-of-highly-effective-characters)).
  - A solo founder with no ad budget won't get that payoff in year one.
  - The studies are also System1's own marketing, so they aren't neutral.

### AI-made images and trust
| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 47% say their trust drops once they realise copy was AI-written. 36% have punished a brand in the last 6 months for seeming "too AI". | SegmentOS, June 2026, 1,017 US adults ([PR.com](https://www.pr.com/press-release/971818)), a vendor survey |
| **18–28 year olds punish brands for AI the most (59%)**. That's the TikTok crowd we want. | same |
| People earning $120k+ are the most willing to pay more for human-made (68%). This is the number Tim Gabe quoted. | same |
| 7 in 10 trust ads made fully by humans, 4 in 10 when AI tools were used, 2 in 10 when AI made it alone | [Harris Poll UK](https://www.theharrispoll.co.uk/insights/blog/the-authenticity-trade-off-how-ai-is-reshaping-trust-in-advertisingnbsp) |
| Some studies find little penalty for AI product photos (only apparel dropped) | [St. Mary's 2025](https://commons.stmarytx.edu/msrjs/38) |

- **Bottom line:** the evidence is mixed, but the risk is real for **characters** and for **young audiences**. A plush 3D GPT render is exactly the look people now spot as AI.

## 4. Risks specific to Ditch

1. **The AI-made look in a money app**
   - Our raccoon is generated with GPT Image in a plush 3D style. That look now reads as "made by AI" to many people under 30.
   - In a money app this does double damage. It looks low-effort (Tim Gabe: "fine is the floor"), and it raises the question "is this a serious tool?"
   - Fixing it means paying a human illustrator or animator for a proper character system. That's real money before launch.
2. **The mascot fights the numbers for attention**
   - Our best moment is the total reveal ("That's $1,284 a year").
   - A surprised raccoon holding a receipt on that screen splits the user's eye between the character and the number.
   - The number is what makes people act and pay.
3. **It looks childish to the people who pay**
   - Our likely buyer earns over $50k and uses an iPhone.
   - Bobby's power users and Monarch/Copilot users are design-sensitive adults.
   - In money apps, a full-time cute character is mostly seen in **kids'** apps (Greenlight, Kit) and the cash-advance end of the market (Dave). Neither is where we want to sit.
4. **It's not ours alone**
   - BitePal (by Reface) is a big raccoon "virtual pet" app, and its raccoon also lives in a bin (see `bold-mascots.md`).
   - Goodfood has "Richard the Raccoon", and Rocket Raccoon exists.
   - A raccoon in a subscription app will get "is this the BitePal guy?" comments. That fails Tim Gabe's test of "one thing only you could have".
5. **The cost of keeping it consistent**
   - The current plan needs 8 onboarding poses, a paywall loop, 2 empty states, a Ditched animation and 3 widget moods. That's 14+ assets.
   - Every new screen later needs a new pose.
   - AI character drift (eyes, scarf, colours shifting) is the classic problem, and fixing it by hand is slow.
   - It also adds app size and the extra Reduce Motion work for each loop.
6. **Notifications can't carry it**
   - Our #1 promise is the reminder, and on the lock screen a reminder is: app icon + "Netflix charges $15.49 in 3 days".
   - The mascot is invisible at the moment that matters most.
   - iOS can attach an image to a notification, but most people never expand it.
7. **The paywall**
   - Our honest-paywall rules need calm and clarity.
   - A character there pulls attention away from the trial timeline and the close button.
   - None of the top-grossing finance paywalls I saw uses one (Monarch, Copilot, Rocket Money).
8. **App Store screenshots**
   - What I saw in the finance category: the top apps show their own screens under a big headline, and Copilot shows real brand logos.
   - A raccoon *could* help us stand out among other subscription trackers. That's an honest plus.
   - But it also tells a stranger "toy" in under a second, and the first 3 screenshots decide most installs.
   - I couldn't load the listings' screenshots directly in this session, so treat this point as a judgement call to check by eye.

### What we'd lose by dropping it (being fair)
- Some **warmth** in a stressful topic. We have to get that back through copy and motion.
- An easy **TikTok character**. But a character only works on TikTok if someone films it every week. Faceless formats ("I found $X of subscriptions I forgot", screen recordings with real logos) suit a solo founder better.
- A ready-made **app icon idea and trademark mark**. We'd need a strong wordmark or symbol instead.
- The work and approvals already done (MASCOT-ROLES, the Higgsfield playbook). That cost is already spent. It shouldn't decide what we do next.

## 5. Options, scored

Scores are out of 10 for Ditch's success. Build cost: 10 = cheapest.

| | Conversion | Trust | Retention | Memorability | Screenshot appeal | Build cost | **Overall** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Full raccoon** (onboarding, paywall, empty states, widget) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 5 | 3 | **5** |
| **B. Small raccoon** (empty states, Ditched celebration, icon only; never near money or the paywall) | 7 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | **6.5** |
| **C. No mascot**: real logos, numbers, the total reveal, Cleo-style voice | 7 | 9 | 6 | 4 | 7 | 9 | **6.5** |
| **D. Signature device instead of a creature**: a "DITCHED" stamp/flick plus voice | 8 | 9 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 7 | **8** |

### A. Full raccoon: 5/10
- **For:** the most memorable on day one, and good TikTok material if someone makes the content.
- **Against:**
  - A cute AI render sits on the screens where trust and money matter most (total, paywall).
  - It looks like BitePal.
  - It costs the most to keep consistent.
  - It's invisible in notifications.

### B. Small raccoon: 6.5/10
- **For:**
  - Keeps warmth at low-stakes moments: the empty list, "Ditched!".
  - That's the same place Monzo and Cleo use spot drawings.
- **Against:**
  - A character that appears twice is hard to remember (System1's whole point is *consistency*).
  - The icon question stays open: a cute raccoon icon still says "toy" in search results.
  - The AI-render problem is still there unless it's redrawn by a human.

### C. No mascot: 6.5/10
- **For:**
  - Looks exactly like the top-grossing peers. Highest trust, cheapest to build.
  - Real logos give colour and recognition for free, as Copilot's welcome shows.
- **Against:**
  - Risks looking like every other clean finance app.
  - Weakest memorability.
  - Nothing that's ours alone except the copy.

### D. Signature brand device: 8/10 (the pick)
**What it is.** One move that is **only Ditch**, repeated everywhere:
- **The Ditch.** On a subscription card, the user swipes or taps "Ditch it". The card tilts and slides off, a teal **DITCHED** stamp lands with a firm haptic, and the line "$15.49/mo back" counts up. This is our Duolingo "ding".
- **The total reveal.** On onboarding screen 3 the yearly total counts up in big tabular numbers, then settles. It's already planned. Make it the hero.
- **Real logos as the illustration.** The welcome screen is a fan or stack of real service logos (Netflix, Spotify, iCloud, ChatGPT…), the way Copilot and Orbit do it. It's instantly recognisable, and it *is* the product.
- **The voice.** Short, dry and on your side, in every screen and notification. For example:
  - "Netflix renews in 3 days. $15.49. Still watching?"
  - "Ditched. That's $186 a year back."
- **The stamp as a mark.** The same DITCHED stamp shape can be the app icon motif, the widget accent and the App Store screenshot device. It gives us one visual hook across every surface.

**Why it wins for our buyer:**
- **Competence first.** Everything on screen is a real number, a real logo or a real date. That fits Aaker's finding, and it's how the top-grossing finance apps look.
- **Warmth through behaviour, not a face.** A satisfying haptic, a kind line of copy and a celebration that counts real money back. That's Tim Gabe's "evidence of care", and his "competence feedback works" point (you see what you saved).
- **It survives every place users meet us:**

| Where | What they see |
|---|---|
| First session on a phone | Real logos, then their own total, then "I'll remind you". No character in the way. |
| App Store screenshots | Big headline + their total + the DITCHED stamp. It reads as "this saves me money" in one second. |
| Notifications | The voice and the stamp-style icon. The mascot couldn't show up here anyway. |
| Widget | Next charge + logo + days left. A worried raccoon tells you less than "Spotify · 2 days · $11.99". |

- **Cheaper:** one Reanimated animation and a few SVGs, built once, with no character drift. It also respects Reduce Motion easily (show the stamp, skip the slide).
- **Clearly ours:** "Ditch it" is our name and our verb. Nobody else can own the stamp.

**What it costs us honestly:**
- Memorability rests on the stamp and the copy being *really* good. A generic checkmark won't do it.
- Budget design time for the stamp shape (hand-drawn, slightly rough, not a font) and the haptic timing.

### How the raccoon could come back (optional, later)
- Keep it **off the bench only if** the owner really loves it. If so:
  - it appears **only** in the two empty states and maybe a rare Ditched variant
  - it's a **flat, hand-finished vector** (by a human illustrator, or at least redrawn by hand)
  - it's never near a price, the total or the paywall
- Decide with evidence after launch. For example, test App Store screenshots with and without it using Apple's Product Page Optimization. That's free and built into App Store Connect.
