# Price hike alerts: what real people say

Research for Ditch, done Nov 20, 2026. Question: do people actually want to be told when a subscription's price goes up?

**How this was done.** Web search plus direct opening of each page (WebFetch), the Hacker News comment API, and Apple's public App Store review feeds (US/GB/CA/AU, ~500 most recent and most helpful reviews per app) for Rocket Money, Bobby, Hiatus, Orbit, Chronicle, Copilot, Monarch and several smaller trackers. **Reddit could not be opened directly from this environment** (reddit.com blocks it, and mirror sites were not allowed). Reddit voices below come only from news articles that quote Reddit, and they are marked that way. Each source was opened unless it says **[unverified]**.

Dates are the post or publish date. "Older" marks anything before 2024.

**Pattern.** Most companies *do* send an email. People still feel blindsided because (a) the email lands in spam or gets buried, (b) it comes a month ahead and is forgotten by the time the charge hits, (c) an intro or student price ends quietly (Adobe #3), or (d) the increase is small and adds up over time (#7). Apple already warns App Store subscribers by email, push and an in-app notice, and needs opt-in for bigger increases (https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT213252, published Dec 13, 2023). So the gap is mostly subscriptions billed **outside** the App Store (Netflix, Spotify, Adobe, Squarespace, phone, gym) and yearly plans.

## 3. Complaints and limits of existing price-increase alerts

| # | Source | Date | Finding | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | Rocket Money (own article) | Jul 6, 2026 | Alerts only fire when the increase is "more than $1.99" **and** "more than five" percent, and only "once you securely connect your financial accounts." So a $1 Spotify bump or a $1.50 bump on a $10 plan never triggers. | (same as #20) |
| 23 | Origin blog | Apr 21, 2026 | Rocket Money and Truebill are "more passive than proactive"; most apps "leave the interpretation to you," so people often notice increases months later. | (same as #21) |
| 24 | Finny blog, "Apps That Track Subscription Price Increases in 2026" | Sep 6, 2026 | "The alert fires after you have paid the higher price at least once." Apps "miss annual plans by default." Rocket Money, Monarch, Copilot and PocketGuard all need a bank link. A changed billing name makes an increase look like a cancellation plus a new subscription. | https://getfinny.app/blog/apps-track-subscription-price-increases-2026 |
| 25 | Finly help page | Nov 18, 2026 | Shows how hard it is to avoid noisy alerts: it needs a steady old price, an increase of at least $0.50 and 3%, confirmation by a second charge in some cases, at most one alert per subscription every 60 days, and it skips bills that change monthly and increases over 75%. Paid plan only. | (link in section 2) |
| 26 | Rocket Money App Store review (2★) | May 15, 2023 (older) | Bank-link worry: "there is otherwise no way to regulate if they take out more than expected or quietly raised the prices without notice." | App Store review feed, app id 1130616675 |
| 27 | Rocket Money reviews collected on JustUseApp | undated | "RocketMoney kept loosing its connection with my bank account." / "my account has been continuously getting un-synced." / "RocketMoney is displaying the incorrect amount for my next bill" / "some transactions that are recurring… aren't recognized". A broken bank sync means a missed price alert. | https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1130616675/rocket-money-bills-budgets/reviews |
| 28 | Monzo Community replies to #18 | Aug 27, 2019 (older) | Why bank apps struggle: "Monzo don't know the amount until it is debited." / "How can you predict it when the amount can change at any time?" / "a lot of care will be needed to get it right enough to be useful." | (same as #18) |

**Pattern.** Every existing alert works **after the fact from bank charges**. That means a bank link (which Ditch's users avoid), at least one higher charge already paid, weak coverage of yearly plans, and thresholds that skip small increases. I found **no** reviews specifically complaining that Rocket Money's price alerts were wrong or noisy. The complaints are about bank sync breaking and wrong bill amounts, which hurt the alert indirectly. A warning **before** the charge, with no bank link, from a public price list matched to what the user entered, is not offered by the big apps I checked.

**Key nuance for Ditch.** The best direct number (Chargebee) says only **6%** didn't notice a hike, so "you'll never notice" is not true for most people. The real pain is **noticing too late** (after the charge, after the email is buried), **creep** across many subs, and **anger** (73% frustrated, 85% feel taken advantage of). About 1 in 5 cancel after a hike (Bango 22%, Chargebee 22%). Those are exactly the people who need a heads-up *before* the renewal.

## Strength of demand: **Moderate**

**Why not strong:**
- Organic, explicit requests ("please add price-change alerts") are thin: one formal feature request with 7 votes, one Bobby review (2023), one Monzo thread (2019), a few reviews praising it. No large upvoted threads found (Reddit not directly searchable here, so this may undercount).
- Most people *do* eventually notice (90%, Chargebee). Apple already warns for App Store subscriptions.
- No one praises or slams Rocket Money's price alert specifically. It doesn't look like a feature people choose an app for.

**Why not weak:**
- The emotion is big and current: 73% frustrated (Deloitte), 85% feel taken advantage of (Hub), 65% call hikes unfair (EY UK). 2025–2026 brought a steady run of hikes (Netflix, Spotify ×3 since 2023, YouTube Premium, Peacock ×4, Xbox +50%, Apple TV+, M365 with a regulator lawsuit).
- "I didn't get an email" complaints repeat in 2025–2026 (Adobe ×3, YouTube, Spotify).
- Supply is moving: Rocket Money now advertises price-increase warnings in its App Store copy, Finly launched them, and many new indie trackers list them. Two companies write SEO pages for "is there an app that tells me when my subscription price increased", which points to real search volume.
- Every existing alert needs a bank link and fires *after* you've paid. A **no-bank-link, before-the-charge** warning is a real gap, and it fits Ditch's privacy position.

**What this means in practice:** it works better as a **retention and "Ditch has my back" feature, and as marketing copy**, than as a main reason to download. It also fits the existing reminder ("Netflix renews in 3 days. $17.99. Still watching?" can become "Netflix went up to $19.99. Renews in 3 days. Still worth it?"). It's a reasonable Pro perk, but research doesn't support it as the headline feature.

Copy directions these support (drafts only, not tested): "Netflix went up. You'll know before it charges." / "Prices creep. Ditch notices." / "When did this become $19.99? Ditch tells you." (Watch the honesty rules: only claim it for services Ditch actually tracks in its public price list, and never imply automatic detection from the user's bank.)
