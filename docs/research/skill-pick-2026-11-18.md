# Which skill should the duck teach? (Nov 18, 2026)

Question: a "Duolingo for one skill" app starring the duck (white, tangerine bill, sunflower / ink / cream). Which skill earns the most for a solo, non-technical maker who builds with Claude?

AppKittie numbers are **model estimates** of App Store gross per month. Treat them as ranks, not bank statements. Two checks on how good they are: Kalam is estimated at ~$201k, and a broker listing that matches it (Arabic, AI practice, launched June 2025) reports **$275k last month**, so that one is close. Wellspoken is estimated at ~$115k, but its founder reported ~$3k in its first 3 months and "doubled twice" by March 2026, so small newcomers can be overstated about 10×. Budget used: AppKittie **191 credits** (1,220 → 1,411). DataForSEO **$0**: the account is paused ("unusual activity"), so Google numbers below come from earlier cached calls only, and there is no UK data.

## 1. Short answer
- **#1 pick: History, told by the duck (overall 7.0).** "5-minute true stories from history, with the sources shown." There is one real leader, Paladin (~$116k/mo, Sep 2024), and it is burning ~$300k a month on Meta ads while **80 of its last 100 US reviews are 1–3★**: "AI slop", "AI gives wrong facts", "nothing like the ads", hidden prices, charged after cancelling, a rate-us prompt 15 times. The next history app, Epictok, is 87% 1–3★ and earns ~$4k. The App Store keyword is easy ("learn history" popularity 45, difficulty 17, the lowest difficulty we measured). Claude can genuinely write the content, the duck fits as narrator, and history is native to TikTok. Duolingo has shown no sign of adding history.
- **Why not the obvious bigger markets?** The biggest 2025–26 "Duolingo for X" wins are **identity languages Duolingo ignores or does badly**: ASL (Lingvano ~$524k, Ziggy ~$205k from July 2026), Arabic (Kalam ~$201k–275k from June 2025) and Irish (HeyRua ~$157k from Aug 2026, $0 on ads). They pay best, but they need native speakers or Deaf signers on audio and video, and the duck can't sign or recite. ASL is now crowded, and Kalam has Arabic.
- **Honest #2: a US citizenship test coach (6.5).** It makes money (leader ~$171k/mo), the content is free and public, and USCIS's new 128-question test (for anyone filing from Oct 20, 2025) left the leader's app outdated in reviews. But people leave once they pass, so it's a lifetime-purchase business, not a streak app.
- **#3 is conditional: Quranic Arabic (6.0, rising to ~7.5 *if* you are Muslim and have an Arabic-literate reviewer).** Demand is huge ("learn quran" 66, "learn arabic" 80), and the comprehension niche is weak (Quranic ~$10k, buggy). But a cartoon duck in a Quran app is a real mismatch, and mistakes there are unforgivable.

## 3. Deep cards

### #1 History, told by the duck ("Ditch the textbook" voice)
**What it is:** one true story a day in 5 minutes: 6–8 swipeable cards (art + 2 lines each), then 3 quick questions, with streaks, levels and "seasons". Every story ends with a **Sources** card (2+ links), the same promise AnkiDecks makes. The duck is a dry, time-travelling narrator who appears at the start and end and never covers the story.
**Why it wins the gap:** Paladin's reviews ask for exactly what we would build anyway. They want the story without quizzes every 90 seconds, a visible price, no billing traps, true facts and no AI look. Our honest-paywall rules (price on screen, × from frame one, a real reminder before the trial ends) are the product here. Kalam, Vocal Image and Tahanan also get 1★ reviews for "no reminder before the trial charged me".
**First 10 lessons ("Season 1: History's worst ideas"):** 1 The Emu War (1932, Australia loses to birds; the duck is smug) · 2 The Dancing Plague of 1518 · 3 The Great Stink of 1858 (London's sewers) · 4 Tulip Mania 1637 (economics, sneaked in) · 5 The Year Without a Summer, 1816 (→ *Frankenstein*) · 6 The Radium Girls · 7 Napoleon wasn't short (propaganda) · 8 The Boston Molasses Flood · 9 Weimar money in wheelbarrows (economics again) · 10 Stanislav Petrov, the man who didn't start WW3.
**Content cost:** low. Claude drafts. You check each story against 2 sources (~45–60 min a story), and the art is public-domain paintings from Wikimedia/the Met plus a fixed set of duck poses. Launch with 60 stories (2 months of daily lessons), then add 3–4 a week. Licensing is easy: public-domain images only, no AI imagery (reviewers hate it).
**Moat and emotion:** a mid-level identity ("the friend who knows the story"). People stay for the series, for streaks, and for sharing ("send this to someone"). The weakest part is that anyone can copy the format, so the moat is a trusted voice plus sources.
**Pricing:** free = today's story + the whole first season. Pro = the full library, audio narration, a widget and offline: **$34.99/yr (7-day trial) · $5.99/mo · $69.99 lifetime**. Paladin charges $49.99–$99/yr, so we undercut and show the price up front.
**Growth:** TikTok/Reels/Shorts: each lesson becomes a 45-second duck-narrated video ("History's dumbest moment, day 14"). That's free distribution, because the lesson *is* the video. ASO: "learn history" (45/17), "history facts", "history app", "on this day". Paladin's Meta ads are teaching the market for us.
**Year 1 (net after Apple's 15%):** bear ~$150/mo by month 12 (~$1k total: no video hits) · base ~$2.5k/mo by month 12 (~$12–15k total: 3k downloads/mo × ~$1) · bull ~$25k/mo (~$100k+: one series goes viral, 20k downloads/mo × Paladin's $1.26/download).
**Risks:** a factual error goes viral (the Sources card and a "report an error" button are the fix); Paladin and Nibble copying "honest" positioning; history skews older (Paladin's buyers come from Facebook). Duolingo: added Math and Music (2023) and Chess (2025), with no history signal, but it could.

### #2 US citizenship interview coach
**What it is:** the duck gets you through the naturalization interview: the 2025 civics test (128 questions, up to 20 asked, 12 needed), the reading and writing test, and N-400 interview English. You speak the answers out loud.
**Gap:** reviews of the leader (Citizenship Test Plus): it crashes after updates, a paid ad-free version won't restore, and one user wrote "only has the 100 question version" after Oct 20, 2025. The flashcards also repeat the same questions.
**First 10 lessons:** 1 How the interview works · 2 The Constitution and "We the People" · 3 The Bill of Rights · 4 The three branches · 5 Congress (100/435) + find *your* senators · 6 President, VP, cabinet · 7 Rights and responsibilities · 8 Colonies to 1776 · 9 Civil War and Lincoln · 10 First mock interview (spoken, with the yes/no N-400 words).
**Content cost:** very low. USCIS material is public domain; Claude writes the explanations; text-to-speech handles audio; Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese and Tagalog help comes later. **Pricing:** all 128 flashcards free; **$19.99 one-time** "Interview Ready" (mock interviews, reading/writing, audio, test-day plan). **Growth:** ASO ("citizenship test", "civics test 2025"); YouTube Shorts in learners' languages; free materials for ESL nonprofits. **Year 1:** bear ~$400/mo · base ~$3–4k/mo · bull ~$30k/mo. **Risks:** policy changes (USCIS edits answers; politics); must say "not affiliated with USCIS" (Apple rejects government look-alikes); short lifetime per user; the audience isn't the duck's usual 23–40 crowd.

### #3 Quranic Arabic (only if founder fit is real)
**What it is:** understand what you recite, one verse a day: word by word, root by root, starting from Al-Fatiha. Swap the duck for a calm, letter-based visual style.
**Gap:** Quranic (2018, ~$10k) is loved but buggy: audio doesn't match the words, progress is lost, support is slow. Kalam's reviews: AI mispronunciation and no trial reminder. Muslims already pay Tarteel ~$333k and Quranly ~$100k.
**First 10 lessons:** 1 Bismillah · 2 Rabb and al-'alamin (root r-b-b) · 3 Ar-Rahman ar-Rahim (r-h-m) · 4 Pronouns huwa/anta/nahnu · 5 "Iyyaka na'budu" ('-b-d) · 6 Sirat al-mustaqim · 7 Al-Ikhlas word by word · 8 min / fi / 'ala / ila · 9 The words you say in salah · 10 Review: Al-Fatiha and Al-Ikhlas fully understood.
**Content cost:** high trust cost. You need a qualified reviewer, and you need licensed recitation audio and translation (Sahih International is copyrighted; the Quranic Arabic Corpus is GPL). **Pricing:** $29.99/yr · $4.99/mo · $79.99 lifetime. **Growth:** a "One Quran word a day" account; Ramadan (around Feb 2027) is the launch moment; ASO "learn quran" (66/28). **Year 1:** bear ~$300/mo · base ~$3k/mo · bull ~$30k/mo. **Risks:** religious errors, sectarian disputes, and the mascot mismatch.

## 4. Blunt verdicts on the four you asked about
- **Economics: no.** "economics" scores 57 on the App Store, but nobody pays for an economics-lesson app (no earner found). Put economics *inside* history as money stories (Tulip Mania, Weimar, the South Sea Bubble).
- **Personal finance / investing: no, for us.** It isn't $0 as last round thought: Finelo makes ~$311k/mo (104k ratings). But it sells trading dreams through YouTube ads ("let AI bots trade for me"), which our honesty rules forbid. Calm money-literacy apps earn about nothing (financial-literacy search results $0, Zogo is funded by banks, Bloom's in-app revenue shows $0). "learn investing" is only 27/14, and Google "learn about investing" fell 50%. Money lessons fit Ditch, not a new app.
- **History: yes, #1.** One leader, ad-funded and badly reviewed, easy keywords, cheap content Claude can write, and the best fit for the duck. Weakness: no 2025–26 history newcomer has hit big yet (Epictok $4k, Fragmenta $0.6k), so the Ziggy signal here is Paladin (2024), not a 2026 app.
- **General micro-learning (Imprint, Headway, Nibble, Brilliant, Elevate): no.** It's big money ($150–600k each), but it belongs to studios spending heavily on Meta ads and selling on the web. It needs endless breadth of content, and "bite-sized learning" newcomers earn $0. History is the focused wedge of this market. Win it first, then add seasons on other topics.

## 5. What else I learned
- **The Ziggy pattern is "identity language Duolingo neglects + speak/sign from lesson 1 + a hard paywall + TikTok/influencers"**: Ziggy (no Meta ads), HeyRua ($0 Meta ads, +50% revenue in 30 days, fox mascot), Kalam (influencers + Meta + TikTok ads; 8–10% conversion, $33–37 LTV per the listing). All three get 1★ reviews for paywalling from lesson 1 and for buggy AI or video. If you ever have a native-speaker co-founder, this is the highest-paying shape.
- **Ziggy may be peaking:** AppKittie shows downloads −53% and revenue −39% in the last 30 days.
- **App Store search isn't what drives these apps:** "learn sign language" is only 21 popularity, yet two apps make $700k+ from it. Social video drives them. So weigh TikTok fit above keywords.
- **Hot 2026 newcomers in France** (Clarté speaking coach, Sophia general knowledge, Dialect heritage languages, Teorify driving test in Spain) show model values of exactly $1.00 per download, which looks like a placeholder. They are a signal of category heat only.

## 6. Caveats
- AppKittie revenue misses web sales (Paladin, Headway and Nibble bill via Stripe as well), and it can be about 10× off for small newcomers (see Wellspoken).
- No fresh Google data (DataForSEO paused). Google figures are from Nov 18 cached calls; UK is missing.
- Review samples are the 100 most recent US reviews. Recent reviews skew negative for ad-funded apps.
- Year-1 ranges are judgment built from $/download ratios, not forecasts.
