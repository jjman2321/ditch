# Free tools, guides and programmatic pages for ditchsubs.app (Nov 13, 2026)

Research only: no project files changed. This builds on `docs/website/audit-2026-11-13/audit-seo.md` and doesn't repeat it. Where a guide was already on the audit's list, I mark it **(audit)** and only add what's new.

## How I measured demand (read this first)

- **Ahrefs and Similarweb aren't connected, so there are no search-volume numbers.** I used three signals instead:
  1. **Google autocomplete** (pulled live, US English, ~200 seed phrases). A phrase only shows up in autocomplete when enough people type it. Many long variants ("…reddit", "…2026", "…uk", "…for students", a specific amount like "…9.99") mean steady demand.
  2. **Who ranks now** (web search, top results). Forum threads, dated news articles and throwaway `*.pages.dev` / `*.vercel.app` sites in the top 10 mean the query is **winnable** for a new site. Apple, Google, banks and big publishers mean it's **hard**.
  3. **Reddit and Apple Community threads** asking the same question again and again.
- Ratings: **Demand** H / M / L. **SERP strength** (how strong the current results are) Weak / Mixed / Strong. **Fit** = can we build it honestly with our own data, client-side only.

## What the search results look like in this niche (the big picture)

1. **The results are flooded with cheap, auto-made "tool" sites.** For almost every generic calculator query ("subscription cost calculator", "annual vs monthly", "subscription audit checklist", "streaming cost per hour"), the top 20 includes 5–10 sites on `pages.dev`, `vercel.app` or `onrender.com` (subscan, quietmoneyleaks, statementdecoder, calcplanet, subscrub, subtrakr…). Two things follow:
   - **The bar is low.** A new domain with a real tool and real data can outrank them.
   - **Google is removing these sites.** Google's spam updates in March, August and September 2026 went after "scaled content abuse" (many near-identical pages made to rank) ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/programmatic-seo-after-march-2026-surviving-scaled-content-ban), [gsqi Aug 2026 case studies](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/), [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)). That helps us if we stay small and data-led. It hurts us if we copy their "one page per keyword" pattern.
2. **SubTracker is the one real tool competitor.** It has 6 free tools: cost calculator, annual vs monthly, streaming cost per hour, free-trial reminder, family split, cancel deadline ([subtracker.io/tools](https://subtracker.io/tools)). They're generic: you type your own prices, the currencies are USD/EUR/GBP/CHF/CAD/AUD only, and the "Service" box on the trial tool does nothing. **Our edge is real, checked prices already filled in, in 17 countries including the Gulf, Egypt and India**, plus iPhone-specific facts.
3. **RecurDash** (guides, US only) and **Subger** (40 languages, auto-translated) own many "X vs Y" and "price increase" pages. Neither has a "what is this charge" tool.
4. **Forums rank for the most anxious questions.** "Why is Apple charging me 9.99", "can I cancel a free trial before it charges", "cancel Apple subscription without an Apple device", "APPLE.COM/BILL 866-712-7753": the top results are `discussions.apple.com` threads. Forums ranking is the clearest sign a careful, sourced page can win. These are also our exact users: worried about a charge, on an iPhone.
5. **Calculators are invisible to AI search unless the answer is also in the page HTML.** ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read the page without running JavaScript. A tool that only shows numbers after a click gives them nothing to quote. **Every tool page needs a server-rendered "answer table" and a worked example under the tool** (for example "In the US, $2.99 a month from APPLE.COM/BILL is usually iCloud+ 200 GB. Checked Nov 9, 2026 on support.apple.com"). That table is what gets cited.

## 3. Programmatic pages: which are worth it, which are penalty bait

The rule I'd use: **a page only exists if it carries numbers or facts a searcher can't get from the service's own page in one click, and every number is sourced and dated.** Same as the freshness gates the site already has (90-day banner, 180-day noindex).

| Pattern | Unique data? | Count | Penalty risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Charge finder hubs:** `/charges/apple-com-bill`, `/charges/amazon-digital`, `/charges/google`, `/charges/paypal`, `/charges/netflix`, `/charges/spotify`, `/charges/microsoft`, `/charges/openai` | **Yes.** Each lists every amount we've checked for that biller, by country and plan, with sources ("$2.99 = iCloud+ 200 GB; $19.99 = ChatGPT Plus via iPhone…") | ~8–10 | **Low.** Each page is a big, distinct table | **Do it.** **Don't** make one page per amount (`/charges/apple-9-99`). That's exactly the scaled pattern Google removes. Amounts live as anchors (`#9-99`) in the hub's table |
| **"[service] price in [country]"** (separate URL from the hub) | **Yes for the Gulf, Egypt and India.** Local currency, VAT basis, billed-in-USD quirks, carrier billing, App Store vs web price, change history | Start with ~25 (Netflix, Spotify, ChatGPT Plus, iCloud, YouTube Premium, PS Plus × AE, SA, EG, IN, plus QA/KW where checked) | **Medium.** Fine if gated; the risk is copying the hub's table | **Do it, gated.** Publish only when: the row is verified, it has ≥1 country-only fact (tax, billing currency, carrier, a price change), and it's ≤90 days old. ## 5. Build rules for every tool (SEO, AI search, honesty)

- **Server-render the answer.** Under each tool: a short answer (40–60 words, dated, sourced), a real HTML table of the underlying prices, and one worked example in plain text. That's what Google snippets and AI answers quote; the interactive part is for people.
- **One tools hub:** `/tools` listing all tools with a one-line answer each; add it to the footer, sitemap and llms.txt (with a one-line note per link, as the audit suggested).
- **Schema:** `WebApplication` (applicationCategory FinanceApplication, offers price 0, `isAccessibleForFree`) + `BreadcrumbList`; `Dataset` on the Charge finder hubs and the price-change log. Use the same `@id` publisher and author graph the audit recommends. No FAQ rich-result chasing.
- **Freshness:** tools inherit the price data's checked dates and the 90/180-day gates. Show "Prices checked Nov 9–12, 2026" next to every result.
- **Honesty (CLAUDE.md rules 4–5):**
  - Charge finder: "possible matches", "only Apple / Amazon / Google can confirm", and the official link each time. Never "we detected" or "we found your subscription".
  - Calculators: "a year you won't pay" only in the ditch context. Never "saved" or "you save".
  - Apple One / split tools: show both numbers and let the user decide. Don't nudge.
  - Trial tool: "Ditch reminds you before it charges", never "Ditch cancels it".
- **Privacy:** nothing typed into a tool leaves the browser. No analytics on what people pick. Say so in one line under each tool ("Runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere"). It's true, and rivals make the same claim, so we must too.
- **Mobile first:** these queries come from phones (people look at their banking app, then search). Keep the tool and answer above the fold on a 390-wide screen, with 44 pt targets.
- **Copy voice:** short and dry, on the user's side ("$2.99 from Apple every month is usually iCloud+ 200 GB. Here's how to check.").
